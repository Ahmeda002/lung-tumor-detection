Introduction

CT shows shape and PET shows activity

CT

X-ray based, shows structure

Tumor is a gray spot with a clear edge

Lung is black because it is full of air

Tells us where things are. Says nothing about whether the tissue is active.

PET

Sugar tracer, shows activity

Bright spot where cells burn sugar fast

The heart glows too. Healthy tissue also uses sugar.

Tells us what is active. Blurry about exactly where, and not every glow is cancer.

Research goal

Develop an AI model that combines CT anatomy with PET activity to localize lung tumors better than either scan alone

Measured against a CT only model and a PET only model under the same conditions.

Hypothesis

What we expect to find

Expectation 1

Fusion beats both

CT gives the edges, PET gives the glow. Together: fewer missed tumors and fewer false alarms.

Expectation 2

Biggest gains on hard cases

Small tumors, and tumors against the chest wall that blend in on CT.

Expectation 3

PET alone finds but misplaces

Good at spotting tumors, but looser boxes and false alarms from normal glow like the heart.

If fusion doesn’t win, that is still a result. The comparison is fair, so it tells us which input to trust.

Detailed design · Training data

The model learns from labeled examples

Positive example

Tumor + expert box

The radiologist’s box becomes the answer the model must learn to draw.

Negative example

Tumor-free, no box

Healthy chest slices with empty labels, so a glow like the heart’s isn’t called a tumor.

Learning loop

Guess, compare, adjust

Each miss nudges the weights. This repeats for up to 100 epochs.

About 100 patients to learn from, 13 to tune, 20 held back for the final test.

Detailed design · Evaluation

What counts as finding a tumor

Radiologist’s box

Overlap

Model’s box

IoU is the overlap divided by the total area both boxes cover. At 0.5 or higher we call it a hit.

Precision

Of the boxes the model drew, how many were real tumors. Low precision means false alarms.

Recall

Of the real tumors, how many the model found. Low recall means missed tumors.

F1 and mAP

F1 combines precision and recall. mAP at 50 uses the 0.5 overlap rule. mAP at 50 to 95 averages over stricter ones.

Detailed design · Robustness

Three ways real scans go wrong

Noise

Gaussian and Poisson noise added to copies of the test images, for all three models.

Missing modality

For the fusion model, the PET channel or the two CT channels are set to black.

Misalignment

For the fusion model, the PET channel is shifted by 2, 5 or 10 pixels off the CT.

No retraining. We measure the drop in mAP from the clean test set to each degraded one.
